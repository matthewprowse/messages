# Konva Go To Market Strategy (Draft, 4 November 2026)

A draft for the founder. Numbers come from `docs/PLAN.md` (Business Model, Cost, Revenue and Profit by User Count,
Launch Model, Marketing Plan, Founder Hours) unless marked otherwise. Competitor, CRM, portal and Microsoft or Google
prices, with sources and dates, are in the Appendix: Market and Price Research at the end. Nothing here is agreed until
it is recorded in `docs/PLAN.md`.

All prices exclude VAT. "Cost to serve" means the variable cost of one seat after Gemini's 1 January 2027 price rise,
with the plan's usage mix (25% low, 55% medium, 20% high) and R8 of hosting: about **R105 an Individual seat and R110 a
Team seat**. Today it is about R15 lower.

## The Short Answer

Keep the prices. Change how they are packaged, not how big they are.

- R349 and R499 already give about 70% to 78% gross margin per seat, so the 70% target is met today.
- Dad's R50 to R100 a month (if per user) is at or below what one seat costs to run. There would be nothing left to
  pay for marketing, support or the founder.
- The real fix for "it feels high" is to sell offices, not agents, with a founding price for pilots, an office plan
  for 5 to 25 agents, and a cheaper Lite tier kept in reserve for agents who pay for themselves.

## 1. The Answer to Dad

### Why Konva Is Not a Microsoft 365 Competitor

Microsoft 365 Family is a household utility. Microsoft's own price is R1,999 a year for up to six people today (R1,000 a
year matches a reseller key or an older plan kept from before Copilot was added), which is R14 to R28 a person a month.
It sells storage, Word, Excel and some AI help inside Microsoft's own apps, and only the account holder gets Copilot.
Konva sells something else, to someone else, paid from a different pocket.

The fair comparison for "Copilot on email" is Microsoft's business version, and it is not cheap: Business Basic or
Standard (R159 to R329 a user) plus the Copilot Business add-on (about R297 to R416 a user) comes to **about R460 to R745
a user a month**, for email only, with no WhatsApp, Instagram or portal leads. Konva's R499 Team seat sits inside that
range. Google is the same story: Gemini comes bundled in a R116 to R231 Workspace seat, which tells you generic AI on
email is worth about R33 a month on its own. So Konva must never sell "AI on email". It sells what Copilot and Gemini do
not do.

| | Microsoft 365 Family with Copilot | Konva |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who pays | A household, from the family budget | An estate agency principal, from commission income |
| What it is for | Documents, storage, help writing in Outlook and Word | Never losing a client lead across WhatsApp, email and portals |
| Channels | Microsoft's own apps | WhatsApp, Gmail, Outlook, iCloud, Instagram, LinkedIn, calendars in one client timeline |
| Sorted by | App and folder | Client: one person, one history |
| Drafts | Generic help when you ask | Pre-drafted replies in the agent's own voice, waiting when the lead arrives |
| Who owns the history | The person | The office: when an agent leaves, the clients stay |
| Lead capture | None | Property24 and Private Property enquiries become contacts, tasks and drafts |
| Owner view | None | Unanswered leads and reply times per agent |
| What it is judged against | Google One, iCloud+ | A CRM, portal fees, desk fees and the value of one lost lead |

Copilot helping you write an email in Outlook is a feature. Konva is the place where a client's WhatsApp, emails and
portal enquiry meet, so the office replies first and keeps the client. Nobody buys a CRM because Word has spell check.

### What a Principal Actually Compares It With

| Comparison | Cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Property CRM per agent | R100 to R200 (Propcon R170 a profile; Entegral Base about R100 a user; Prop Data from R908.50 an office with unlimited agents) | The danger zone: a principal paying R170 for a CRM will see R499 as three times that. Never pitch Konva as another CRM; it is a lead reply and client retention tool, closer to Wati or Front |
| WhatsApp business tools (Wati, Respond.io, SleekFlow, Trengo) | R180 to R680 per included user, plus Meta's message fees | Next to these, R499 looks normal |
| Other AI inboxes (Superhuman, Shortwave, Front, Kinso) | R413 to R1,733 a user; Kinso about R974 | R499 is mid range internationally |
| Portal subscriptions (Property24, Private Property) | Office level, no public rate card (Private Property "from R999" is unverified) | The office already pays for leads; Konva makes sure those leads get answered |
| Fixed fees an agent pays the office | RE/MAX about R4,000 a month plus about 6.5% of commission; no general SA desk fee figure found | Konva for one agent is R349 a month. Ask principals in pilot conversations |
| One lost lead | About R60,000 commission | Illustrative: a R1.2M sale at 5%, excluding VAT (SA commissions are usually 5% to 7.5% plus VAT). On a 50/50 split, about R30,000 each to agent and office |

So: Konva for one agent for a year (R4,188) is about **7% of one sale's commission**, or 14% of the agent's half. For a
15 agent office (R7,485 a month, R89,820 a year) it pays for itself with about **one and a half extra sales a year
across the whole office**, or one sale saved for every ten agents.

### If Dad Meant Something Else

"50, 100 grand a month" was dictated and is ambiguous:

- **R50 to R100 a user a month:** below cost. See section 2.
- **R50 to R100 for a whole office a month:** far below cost (a 15 agent office costs about R1,650 a month to run).
- **R50,000 to R100,000 of revenue a month:** that is not a price but a target, and it matches the plan: ten offices by
  month 6 is about 150 seats, about R52,000 at the founding price or R75,000 at list. Worth asking him which he meant.

less than 7% of one commission a year per agent, and the pilot measures whether it works before you pay."

## 2. Pricing Recommendation

### Keep, Cut or Restructure

**Keep the list prices. Restructure the offer around the office.** Do not cut to R50 to R100.

### Gross Margin Is Already Met

From the PLAN.md tables:

| Measure | Now | After 1 January 2027 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blended variable cost per user (before hosting) | R84 | R99 |
| Gross margin on variable cost alone (R389.50 average income) | 78% | 75% |
| Margin including fixed costs, 250 users | 72% | 69% |
| Margin including fixed costs, 500 users | 74% | 70% |
| Margin including fixed costs, 1,000 users | 75% | 72% |
| Worst single case: Individual, high usage | 63% | 63% (held by the $5 AI cap) |

The 70% target is met from about 250 to 500 paying users. Below 100 users margin is 58% to 64% because fixed costs
(hosting, the CASA placeholder) dominate: that is a scale problem, not a price problem.

### Gross Margin Is Not the Marketing Budget

These are two different questions:

- **Gross margin** is what is left of each rand of revenue after the cost to serve (AI, storage, card fees, support,
  hosting). It answers "is each customer profitable to run?" At 70% to 78%, yes.
- **The marketing budget** is paid out of that gross profit. The question for it is "how fast does a customer pay back
  what it cost to win them?" The usual tests are **payback under 12 months (under 6 is good)** and **lifetime gross
  profit at least 3 times the cost to acquire (LTV:CAC of 3 or more)**.

So "we need 70% margins because marketing is expensive" is the right instinct with the wrong label. What matters is
payback on the R1,200 cost of an ad-won user, and the plan's profit tables exclude marketing, salaries and hires.

### Every Price Point, Tested

Payback assumes the plan's middle ad cost to win a user (R1,200). Lifetime assumes 5% monthly cancellations (about 20
months). Card fees scale down with price.

| Price point | Price a month | Cost to serve | Gross profit a month | Gross margin | Payback on R1,200 | Lifetime gross profit | LTV:CAC |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dad, full AI, R50 | R50 | about R98 | minus R48 | loss | never | loss | none |
| Dad, full AI, R100 | R100 | about R99 | about R1 | 1% | never | about R20 | none |
| Lite, R149 | R149 | about R48 | R101 | 68% | 12 months | R2,020 | 1.7 |
| Lite, R199 | R199 | about R50 | R149 | 75% | 8 months | R2,980 | 2.5 |
| Individual, yearly | R279 | about R103 | R176 | 63% | 7 months (R3,348 paid up front) | R3,520 | 2.9 |
| Individual, monthly | R349 | R105 | R244 | 70% | 5 months | R4,880 | 4.1 |
| Founding office seat | R349 | about R106 | R243 | 70% | under 1 month (founder sold) | R4,860 | high |
| Team seat, yearly | R399 | about R108 | R291 | 73% | 4 months | R5,820 | 4.9 |
| Team seat, monthly | R499 | R110 | R389 | 78% | 3 months | R7,780 | 6.5 |

What this shows:

- R50 loses money on every seat, even a light user (R54 today, R67 after January). R100 breaks even at best, and a
  medium or high user (R114 to R134) loses money. Neither can fund a single rand of marketing.
- R349 and R499 pass both tests easily. Prices this healthy are what let a bootstrapped founder buy ads at all.
- An office won by the founder costs very little cash: a free pilot month for 15 seats is about R1,650 of running cost,
  roughly R110 a seat, so it pays back in the first paid month. The constraint on offices is founder hours, not money.

### What to Change

**1. Founding Office price (pilots, first 10 offices only).**
- 30 days free, with the founder setting the office up.
- Then R349 a seat (the Individual price, 30% off Team) locked for 12 months, on a 12 month agreement.
- In return: before and after reply times, a usable quote, a named case study, and introductions to two other
  principals. After 12 months the office moves to the yearly Team price (R399).
- Margin is still 70%. It creates urgency ("10 founding offices") without cutting the public price.

**2. Office plans for 5 to 25 agents (the main offer).** Principals budget in flat amounts, like desk fees. Sell a flat
monthly price on a 12 month agreement, paid by debit order or yearly up front:

| Plan | Seats included | Price a month | Per seat at full use | Margin at full use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small Office | up to 8 | R3,490 | R436 | 75% |
| Office | up to 15 | R5,990 | R399 | 72% |
| Large Office | up to 25 | R8,990 | R360 | 69% |
| Over 25, franchise head office | quoted | from R349 a seat | R349 | 68% |

Monthly per-seat Team (R499, 5 at least) stays as the no-commitment option. Unused seats in a bundle are pure margin;
a growing office moves up a plan, which is easier to sell than seat by seat invoices.

**3. Annual plan.** Keep about 20% off (R279, R399). For offices, push the 12 month agreement hard: a 15 agent office
on yearly Team pays about R71,800 up front, which funds months of building and ads. For individuals, 20% off is
generous; 15% would be normal, but changing it now is not worth the confusion.

**4. Lite tier (design now, launch only if price is the main reason deals are lost).**
- For agents who pay for themselves and balk at R349. Suggested R199 a month (R159 yearly).
- Includes: the client timeline across WhatsApp and email, search, sorting and Junk on the cheap model, portal lead
  capture with the fixed parsers (no AI cost), tasks, calendar.
- Leaves out: pre-drafts, drafts in your voice beyond about 20 a month, the spoken Daily Brief, voice note and meeting
  transcripts. AI cap about $1.50 (R25) a month instead of $5.
- Cost to serve: about R45 to R55 (AI R10 to R25, storage R2, card fees R7, support R10, hosting R8).
- Risk: cannibalising R349. Never offer Lite on Team or Office plans, and show it only as a downgrade when someone
  cancels or on the pricing page once launched. Launch gate: more than 30% of lost deals in months 1 to 6 cite price.
- This is also the honest version of dad's idea: a R100 to R200 product exists, but only without the heavy AI, and only
  through cheap channels (it takes 12 months or more to pay back an ad-won user at R149).

**5. Usage caps.** Keep the $5 AI Budget per seat, pooled for teams. After 1 January 2027, do not raise it to $7.50
for everyone (adds about R20 a seat); raise it for Team and Office plans only if more than 10% of seats hit a feature
cap in a month. Team margin at R499 with the higher cap is still about 74%. Show caps in plain words ("about 300 drafts
a month") rather than percentages on the pricing page.

**6. Price rises.** The model assumes 6% a year. Founding offices are locked for 12 months; everyone else gets 60 days'
notice. It is far easier to discount a price than to raise one, which is the strongest reason not to start at R100.

## 3. Ideal Customer, Buyer and Positioning

### Ideal Customer

A franchise or independent estate agency office with **5 to 25 agents**, where the **principal decides** (RE/MAX,
Seeff, Pam Golding, Chas Everitt, Harcourts franchises and strong independents), that already pays for portal leads, runs
its clients on WhatsApp and email, and has lost an agent (and their clients) in the last two years.

Signals of a good fit: agents share leads from Property24 and Private Property; the principal complains about slow
follow-up; agents use one WhatsApp number for clients and family; the office has had a POPIA scare or a client
complaint about a missed reply.

Poor fit for now: one-agent agencies on tight budgets (side door only), offices that run everything inside a CRM's own
inbox, and anyone whose WhatsApp is their only channel and who cannot accept the Baileys ban risk.

### Buyer and User

| Person | Role in the deal | What they care about | Message |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Principal or franchise owner | Buyer, signs and pays | Leads answered, agents' clients staying with the office, visibility | "When an agent leaves, the clients stay with the office." "See which leads are waiting for a reply." |
| Agent | User and champion (or blocker) | Speed during show days, less admin, their personal chats staying private | "Reply to every lead in minutes, even during a show day." Drafts in their voice are the how. |
| Office administrator | Daily user of shared inboxes | One place for info@ and the office WhatsApp, assigning leads | "Every enquiry assigned to someone, and nobody replies twice." |
| Franchise head office (year 2) | Group buyer | Brand standard for lead response, data staying with the brand | "Lead response you can measure across every office." |
| Brokers and financial advisers (year 2) | Buyer is the practice owner | FAIS records, client history | "A per-client archive of WhatsApp and email for FAIS and POPIA." |

Two things agents must hear early, or they will block the deal: **personal chats stay private** (sharing is per chat,
the principal never sees family chats), and **Konva never sends anything without them**.

### Positioning

Konva is the client inbox for South African professionals who win work by replying first: sorted by client, owned by
the office, made for South Africa. Hero: "Never Keep a Client Waiting." Do not lead with "one inbox" or "AI inbox";
do not make WhatsApp the whole pitch; no "we can't read your messages" until Confidential Space is live (say "encrypted
per user, POPIA aligned").

### Proof Plan: Reply Time Before and After

Proof is the whole early marketing budget. For each pilot office:

1. **Baseline (day 0).** When accounts connect, Konva imports the last 30 days. If response tracking can compute median
   first reply time from that import (to confirm in the code), the "before" number costs the office nothing and is
   ready on day one. Otherwise, take one week of reply times before drafts are switched on.
2. **Measure (days 1 to 30, then monthly).** Median first reply time to a new lead, share of leads answered within 15
   minutes and within 1 hour, leads still unanswered at 6pm, and portal enquiries captured as leads. Counts and timings
   only, never message text.
3. **Report.** A one-page summary for the principal at day 30, then monthly. This is also the renewal conversation.
4. **Publish with permission.** "Office X went from a median of N hours to N minutes" with the principal's quote. No
   made-up numbers before real ones exist.

Outside research says speed matters (a widely cited 2011 Harvard Business Review study found firms that replied within
an hour were about seven times likelier to qualify a lead than those that waited longer), but it is US data. Replace it
with Konva's own pilot numbers as soon as three offices have them.

## 4. Launch Sequence: First 12 Months

### Founder Hours

About 13 hours a week from 2027 (two mornings 6:30 to 8, two evenings 6 to 8, Saturday). Suggested split:

| Activity | Hours a week | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Selling: principal messages, demos, office setups | 5 | Saturday, one early morning slot before sales meetings |

### 6. Price anchoring and per seat vs office pricing

| Evidence | Finding | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anchoring | People's estimates are pulled toward an initial number, even an irrelevant one | Tversky and Kahneman 1974, Science 185(4157), [doi:10.1126/science.185.4157.1124](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124) |
| Arbitrary anchors set willingness to pay | Students' bids for goods moved with the last two digits of their ID numbers; relative prices stayed coherent | Ariely, Loewenstein and Prelec 2003, Quarterly Journal of Economics 118(1), [doi:10.1162/00335530360535153](https://doi.org/10.1162/00335530360535153) |
| Decoy effect | Adding a dominated option raises the share of the option it is close to (about 9% average lift across six categories in the lab) | Huber, Payne and Puto 1982, Journal of Consumer Research 9(1), [doi:10.1086/208899](https://doi.org/10.1086/208899); [Huber et al. 2014 retrospective](https://people.duke.edu/~jch8/bio/Papers/HuberPaynePutoJMR%202014.pdf) |
| Compromise effect | Adding an extreme option increases the share of the middle option | Simonson and Tversky 1992, Journal of Marketing Research 29(3), [abstract](https://casact.org/abstract/choice-context-tradeoff-contrast-and-extremeness-aversion) |
| Showing the expensive plan first | Revenue per visitor up 18% in one case (anecdote, not a controlled study) | [Atticus Li](https://atticusli.com/blog/posts/how-to-use-price-anchoring-on-saas-pricing-pages-without-tricking-buyers/) |
| Per seat is declining | Seat based pricing fell from 21% to 15% of surveyed software companies in a year; hybrid rose from 27% to 41%; flat fee fell from 29% to 22% (240+ companies, April to May 2025) | [Kyle Poyar, Growth Unhinged 2025](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/2025-state-of-b2b-monetization) |
| Seat priced retention | Seat based companies had 98% median net revenue retention vs 108% for usage based (2025 data) | [Aleph and Benchmarkit](https://www.getaleph.com/blog/2026-saas-benchmarks-takeaways) |
| Per seat risk | Customers buy fewer seats to save money, which cuts adoption | [Maxio](https://www.maxio.com/blog/pricing-model-for-software) |
| Flat office example | Basecamp: $299 a month yearly for unlimited users vs $15 a user; breaks even at about 20 users | [Automation Atlas 2026](https://automationatlas.io/answers/basecamp-pricing-2026/) |
| SA property tools | Prop Data and the older Entegral Base price per office with unlimited agents; Propcon prices per profile with volume steps | Section 3c |

**What this means for Konva.** The buyer is anchored before the pitch starts: the dad's anchor is R1,000 a year for
Microsoft 365, a principal's is R170 an agent for Propcon. Konva should set its own anchor first, in this order: the
commission on one lost sale (R90,000), then the business price of Copilot on email alone (about R460 to R745 a user with
the base plan), then Konva. On the pricing page, a higher plan or an office plan shown first makes R499 the middle
option (compromise effect). For offices of 5 to 25 agents, the evidence and the local tools both point to office
pricing or office bands with a seat count included (for example 5, 10, 15 and 25 agent bands) rather than a pure per
seat price: it removes the incentive to leave agents off, matches how Prop Data and Entegral sell, and makes the price
"one sale a year" for the whole office. Keep a per seat price above each band so margin holds as offices grow, and keep
the AI cap so a flat office price cannot be eaten by heavy users.
